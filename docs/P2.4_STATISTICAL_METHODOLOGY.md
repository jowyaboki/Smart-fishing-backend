# P2.4 Statistical Methodology

## 1. Overview

The Empirical Tide Validation Engine computes rigorous error metrics to assess astronomical tide prediction fidelity against sea-level gauge observations.

The residual error $e_i$ for a paired observation $i$ is defined as:
$$e_i = \hat{y}_i - y_i$$
where:
- $\hat{y}_i$ is the astronomical harmonic predicted water level in meters.
- $y_i$ is the empirical observed water level in meters.

Under this convention:
- A positive error ($e_i > 0$) indicates the prediction overestimates the observed sea level.
- A negative error ($e_i < 0$) indicates the prediction underestimates the observed sea level.

---

## 2. Scalar Statistical Metrics

Given $N$ validated pairs:

### 2.1 Bias (Mean Error, ME)
Measures persistent systematic over- or under-prediction:
$$\text{Bias} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} e_i = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} (\hat{y}_i - y_i)$$

### 2.2 Mean Absolute Error (MAE)
Measures the average magnitude of prediction errors without regard to sign:
$$\text{MAE} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} |e_i| = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} |\hat{y}_i - y_i|$$

### 2.3 Root Mean Square Error (RMSE)
Penalizes larger outliers more heavily than MAE, serving as standard hydrographic goodness-of-fit:
$$\text{RMSE} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} e_i^2} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} (\hat{y}_i - y_i)^2}$$

### 2.4 Standard Deviation of Errors ($\sigma_e$)
Reflects the dispersion of the residuals around the mean systematic error (bias):
$$\sigma_e = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} (e_i - \text{Bias})^2}$$
Note: Mathematically, $\text{RMSE}^2 = \text{Bias}^2 + \sigma_e^2$.

### 2.5 Extrema Errors
- **Maximum Absolute Error:** $\max_{i} |e_i|$
- **Minimum Absolute Error:** $\min_{i} |e_i|$

## 3. Tidal Peak Analysis (Height and Timing)

Tidal navigational decisions depend heavily on the accuracy of High Water (HW) and Low Water (LW) slack windows and peak heights.

### 3.1 Peak Extrema Detection
Discrete time series points $(t_i, y_i)$ are analyzed with 3-point local extrema filtering:
- **High Water Peak:** $y_{i-1} < y_i \ge y_{i+1}$
- **Low Water Peak:** $y_{i-1} > y_i \le y_{i+1}$

### 3.2 Peak Matching Algorithm
1. Observed peaks and predicted peaks are sorted chronologically.
2. For each observed peak, the closest predicted peak of the identical type (High or Low) within a search window of $\pm 180$ minutes ($\pm 3$ hours) is matched.
3. Each predicted peak is assigned at most once.

### 3.3 Peak Error Formulations
For each matched peak $k$:
- **Height Error:** $\Delta h_k = \hat{h}_k - h_k$ (in meters)
- **Timing Error:** $\Delta t_k = \frac{t_{\text{pred}, k} - t_{\text{obs}, k}}{60\text{ s}}$ (in decimal minutes)

Summary metrics:
- Mean Peak Height Error: $\frac{1}{M}\sum \Delta h_k$
- Peak Height RMSE: $\sqrt{\frac{1}{M}\sum (\Delta h_k)^2}$
- Mean Peak Timing Error: $\frac{1}{M}\sum \Delta t_k$
- Peak Timing RMSE: $\sqrt{\frac{1}{M}\sum (\Delta t_k)^2}$
